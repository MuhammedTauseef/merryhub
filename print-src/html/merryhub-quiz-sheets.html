🔑 Answer Key

The Big Christmas Quiz — answers and the story behind each one. Quizmasters only!

1. StockingsThe tradition is linked to a legend of St Nicholas leaving gold coins in stockings hung up to dry.
2. RudolphRudolph was created in 1939 for a department-store colouring book before the famous song arrived a decade later.
3. TwelveThe twelve days run from Christmas Day to Epiphany on 6 January — and the gifts in the song add up to 364 presents.
4. MistletoeAn old custom said a berry had to be plucked from the mistletoe with every kiss — when the berries ran out, so did the kisses.
5. GermanyDecorated trees spread across Europe in the 1800s and became hugely popular in Britain during Queen Victoria's reign.
6. Father ChristmasFather Christmas was originally a symbol of good cheer and feasting long before he merged with the gift-giving Santa.
7. The candy caneThe earliest candy canes were plain white sugar sticks; the red stripes and peppermint flavour came later.
8. A one-horse open sleigh'Jingle Bells' was written in the 1850s and was not originally intended as a Christmas song at all.
9. A lump of coalIn some traditions it is not coal but a stick, an onion or a piece of garlic — coal is getting off lightly.
10. The NutcrackerTchaikovsky's ballet premiered in 1892 and was not an instant hit — it became a festive staple decades later.
11. A star (the Star of Bethlehem)Their gifts — gold, frankincense and myrrh — are why three kings are traditional, though the Bible never says how many came.
12. 26 DecemberThe name comes from the old custom of giving 'Christmas boxes' of gifts or money to servants and tradespeople.
