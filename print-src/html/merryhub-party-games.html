🔄 The Left–Right Gift Game

Everyone sits in a circle, holding a wrapped gift. One person reads the story aloud. Every time you hear LEFT, pass your gift to the person on your left; every RIGHT, pass it right. When the story ends, the gift in your hands is yours to unwrap!

It was Christmas Eve at the Berry house, and everything had gone slightly LEFT-field. Mum had put the turkey in the oven and immediately left it — no, LEFT it — while she went RIGHT back to the shops for the cranberry sauce she had LEFT on the list.

“There's no time to lose!” she cried, and drove off — turning LEFT at the lights, then RIGHT at the corner shop, which had exactly one jar of cranberry sauce LEFT. She grabbed it, paid, and hugged the shopkeeper, who was quite surprised but agreed it was the RIGHT thing to do.

Back home, Dad was putting the star on the tree. “A little to the LEFT,” said Gran. “No — RIGHT,” said Grandad. The star went LEFT, then RIGHT, then LEFT again, until the cat knocked it RIGHT off the tree and under the sofa, where it stayed until Boxing Day.

When Mum got back, she put the sauce RIGHT in the middle of the table. “Everything is under control,” she said, just as the smoke alarm went off. The turkey was fine — mostly — and what was LEFT of it was declared delicious by everyone, which was exactly the RIGHT verdict.

And the moral of the Berry family Christmas is this: it doesn't matter if things go LEFT or RIGHT — as long as there's something good LEFT to share at the end of it.

🎲 The Dice Gift Exchange

All you need: one wrapped gift per player, a pile in the middle, and one dice. Fast, loud and gloriously unfair.

  1. Everyone brings a wrapped gift (agree a budget first!) and puts it in the middle pile. Sit in a circle.
  2. Going around the circle, each player takes a gift from the pile — no shaking, squeezing or sniffing. Yet.
  3. Now roll the dice in turn and follow the table below. Play three full rounds.
  4. After round three, unwrap everything in front of you. Whatever you are holding is yours!
⚀Roll a 1: Take a gift from the pile. If the pile is empty, steal any wrapped gift instead.
⚁Roll a 2: Swap your gift with the player on your left.
⚂Roll a 3: EVERYONE passes their gift to the person on their right. Chaos. Embrace it.
⚃Roll a 4: Steal any unwrapped gift. Its owner takes a fresh one from the pile.
⚄Roll a 5: Unwrap your gift! (No gift? Take one from the pile and unwrap that.)
⚅Roll a 6: Pick two other players and make them swap gifts with each other.
🎁 Grown-up in charge? Set a timer for extra drama: when it rings mid-round, the game ends instantly after the current roll — gifts where they lie.

🐘 White Elephant — The Rules

The classic gift-stealing game. Bring a wrapped gift that is funny, odd or delightful (agree a budget first) — the sillier, the better the game.

  1. Set up. All gifts go in one pile. Count the players and put matching numbered slips in a hat — everyone draws one.
  2. Player 1 picks any gift from the pile and unwraps it. Show everyone. Accept the applause.
  3. Each next player may either take a fresh gift from the pile or STEAL an unwrapped gift from someone who went before.
  4. If your gift is stolen, you immediately go again: take from the pile or steal from someone else — but you cannot steal your own gift straight back.
  5. Three-steal freeze: a gift stolen three times in one round is frozen — its holder keeps it for good.
  6. The final twist: after the last player, Player 1 gets one bonus move — keep their gift, or swap it for any unfrozen gift in the room.
  7. When the swapping settles, whatever you are holding is yours. Treasure it. Display it proudly. Regift it next year (we will not tell).
🏆 House rules worth agreeing first: the budget, whether gifts should be funny or genuinely nice, and the prize for “most stolen gift” — we suggest the holder chooses the next Christmas film.
